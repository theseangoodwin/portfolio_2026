There’s something satisfying about having a place to put things. A shelf for the books you keep returning to. A notebook for the thought you haven’t quite worked out. A website for the work that doesn’t fit anywhere else.[^place]

This is a sample essay for trying out the article format. The writing is here to show how a longer piece feels on this site: a quiet page, a comfortable reading column, and room to follow a thought.

## A little less between us

Most of what I want from a personal website is simple. I want to read someone’s ideas, see what they’re working on, and get a sense of how they see the world. I don’t need much else around the edges.

That’s the feeling I want to keep here. The familiar type, the space between paragraphs, the small details that make a page feel cared for. A place that feels connected to the rest of the site, even when the writing takes a different direction.

> A good place to write is a place you want to come back to.

There’s room for asides, too. Sometimes a reference matters, but only if you’re curious. Sometimes the tangent is half the fun.[^notes] You can open a note, read it, and carry on from exactly where you were.

## Keep the useful things

Moving writing to a personal site shouldn’t mean losing the parts that make a newsletter useful:

- A direct way to hear when something new is published.
- Links and references that give an idea more context.
- A permanent address for each piece of writing.
- A page that reads just as well on a phone as it does at a desk.

The format should work for a short observation or a longer essay. A heading when the argument turns. A quotation worth sitting with. An image when it tells you something the words can’t.

## Leave room to return

A personal site can change alongside the person making it. An essay can get a correction. An old idea can get a new footnote. Not everything needs to be finished forever before it has a place here.[^garden]

For now, a page and a few words are enough.
